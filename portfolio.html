Group Structure

Five entities, one operating group

PacinfraX Corporation is a US C-Corp holding company. Each subsidiary is wholly owned and carries a distinct role — capital formation, US project holding, customer orders, regional holding and operations.

US · JCE

PacinfraX Infrastructure Fund I LLC

Wholly owned subsidiary targeting a $100M AUM investment pool from private capital to finance the physical build-out of high-density AI computing facilities, power generation and cooling systems. Aimed at creating thousands of direct and indirect jobs in local US communities, with core projects backed by a strong capital structure, transparent asset security and an experienced development team across industrial zones, renewables and data centers.

US · NCE

PacinfraX Infrastructure LLC

Project holding entity for our multiphase gas-powered 500 MW data center in Texas. Through AI-driven operations and modular scalability, it delivers sustainable, resilient and cost-efficient infrastructure engineered for the intensive demands of AI and high-performance computing workloads.

Hong Kong

PacinfraX Technology Ltd.

A customer-driven trading entity that receives data center orders from neocloud companies — specialized, AI-first cloud providers delivering GPU-as-a-Service rather than general-purpose computing. Unlike traditional hyperscalers, neoclouds focus on dense GPU clusters, ultra-fast InfiniBand networking and power-dense liquid cooling built specifically for training and running large AI models.

Singapore

PacinfraX Singapore Pte. Ltd.

Singapore holding entity owning 100% of PacinfraX Vietnam, the revenue-driven operating company for our data center operations across Southeast Asia.

Vietnam

PacinfraX Vietnam Co. Ltd.

Wholly owned by PacinfraX Singapore. Receives operating revenues from data center operations across several high-tech industrial zones in Vietnam, and holds stakes in four AI data center projects totalling 1,200 MW.

Market Context

Why Vietnam

Cushman & Wakefield’s Asia-Pacific Data Centre Construction Cost Guide, published April 2026, identifies Vietnam as a market to watch in the next wave of regional data center development.

The guide places Vietnam among the region’s more cost-competitive markets, with construction costs ranging from $5.7M to $8.7M per MW and a mid-range of $7.2M per MW, while annual construction cost inflation remains moderate at 3.8%.

Delivery Mechanism

Generation is not a second business. It’s how the data center gets built.

PacinfraX does not develop merchant power projects. Gas, solar and storage exist on our sites for one purpose: to energize compute on our schedule instead of the interconnection queue’s.

Because the campus does not depend on a grid connection to operate, construction begins when equipment and permits allow — not when a transmission study clears.

Firm

Natural gas trigeneration

Carries round-the-clock baseload. Recovered exhaust heat drives absorption cooling, cutting the electrical load that cooling would otherwise draw.

Variable

Solar and wind

Daytime and seasonal generation sited beside the load it serves, displacing fuel cost rather than selling into the market.

Bridging

Battery energy storage

Ride-through, load-following against AI demand swings, and renewable firming. Enters the ancillary market once the campus is interconnected.

Market Focus

Why we anchor in low-cost energy markets

Texas has the demand, the fuel and the cost structure. What it lacks is deliverable power — which is precisely the problem we are built to solve.

  • Power costTexas commercial electricity averages roughly 7 to 9 cents per kilowatt-hour, materially below Northern Virginia, Chicago and the Northeast.
  • Fuel accessEagle Ford and South Texas gas supply supports on-site generation at scale, with pipeline infrastructure already serving the Gulf Coast and export markets.
  • Regulatory postureA single-state grid with a competitive market structure, and an emerging federal framework that treats off-grid data centers as a distinct category.
  • Cost structureLand, labor and construction costs below primary markets, with no state income tax supporting recruitment of operating staff.
  • DemandERCOT’s own long-term forecast anticipates load nearly tripling within the decade. The tenants are already here.
  • Second continentVietnam offers construction at a mid-range of $7.2M per MW with moderate cost inflation, a deep industrial base, and government incentives for high-tech projects — diversifying both delivery risk and market exposure.
How We Develop

A consistent model across every site we pursue

01

We lease land, we don’t buy it

Landowners retain title and take a continuing interest in what gets built. Long-term ground leases with participation, rather than a purchase and a handshake.

02

Gas access decides the site

Distance to a pipeline tap governs both capital cost and fuel price over a thirty-five year operating life. We site against the tap, not the substation.

03

Off-grid first, grid second

Campuses are engineered to run islanded. Interconnection, when it arrives, adds arbitrage revenue — it never gates commercial operation.

04

Phased to demand

50 MW at a time, with land, generation and substation capacity reserved for the phases that follow.

05

Water settled up front

Cooling supply and community impact are resolved before capital is committed, not at the permit hearing. Closed-loop design keeps consumption near zero.

06

Built for AI density

Liquid cooling, high-density power distribution and structural loading specified for GPU racks from the outset — not retrofitted later.
